Comics and manga live in your library like books. They reach your devices the same way: the Kobo sync (as colour fixed-layout books on a colour Kobo), Send to Kindle, or a CBZ download for an iPad.

Getting volumes or issues

  1. Comics → search a series (Manga, or Comics for Western titles) → open it.
  2. Tick the volumes or issues you want and choose the language (and reading direction for manga) → Request.
  3. Each copy found is shown to you on Comics to confirm; Yes to all takes every copy offered in one series at once.
  4. Arrivals are checked (series, number, language, a sensible number of pages) before they go into your library; a file that does not match is held for you.

Following a series

  • Follow volumes: each new volume shows under New for you.
  • Follow chapters, then volumes (manga): each new chapter shows up as soon as it is out, and is downloaded when a copy exists. Chapters go into their own series (e.g. One Piece (chapters)). When the volume that collects them is in your library, Comics offers to remove those chapters (they are pre-ticked; nothing is removed until you say so).

Reading

  • Kobo: a Kobo copy is made automatically when you read on a Kobo (a synced one, or one you ticked on the start page), for its screen; Make Kobo copy on the comic's page if not.
  • Kindle: Send to Kindle on its page (converted for your Kindle on the way).
  • iPhone, iPad, Android: Panels or Chunky (iPad), CDisplayEx or KOReader (Android): add the OPDS catalog {{ cfg.BOOKS_URL }}/opds/, or download the CBZ. Nothing is converted: the app shows the pages as they are, and turning the tablet shows a wide page whole.
  • Wrong comic? Its page → Wrong comic.

How pages are laid out on an e-reader

An e-reader's screen is tall, and comics are not all shaped alike. The library looks at every page of a comic and chooses:

  • Portrait pages (most comics and manga): as they are.
  • Some two-page spreads: each half in turn (in manga order for manga), then the whole spread turned sideways, so the full picture is there too.
  • A landscape book (most pages wide: The Complete Peanuts, art books): never cut. On a Kobo the pages are turned sideways: lock its screen rotation to portrait and turn it. On a Kindle they stay whole and upright: switch it to landscape in its Aa menu.
  • Webtoons (very tall pages): cut into screens, top to bottom.
  • Newspaper dailies (every page a single row of panels): on a Kobo each row becomes two rows that fill the screen, no turning needed; on a Kindle each strip stays whole, across the screen.

Not right for a comic? Its page → Pages on e-readers → choose another layout. It is kept for that comic, for everyone who has it: the Kobo copy is made again and a copy sent to a Kindle follows it.
